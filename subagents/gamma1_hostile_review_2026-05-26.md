# Subagent γ1 Hostile Review

**Returned:** 2026-05-26

## Bottom Line

Do not promote a V2 pan-autoimmune ACSL1 claim yet. Current evidence supports only a constrained marker hypothesis: `ACSL1` is enriched in foamy/MIMS2-like MS lesion myeloid states. It does not yet prove ACSL1 is causal, selectively targetable, repair-safe, or recurrent across autoimmune tissues.

## Strongest Objections

### Methodological

- The core MS signal is vulnerable to marker overfit. Proteomics selected a foamy-lesion feature, then snRNA validation used reconstructed `GPNMB`/`APOE` MIMS2-like states. Excluding `ACSL1` from the state definition does not remove circularity around lipid-loaded inflammatory myeloid biology.
- Bulk proteomics is not cell-specific. Foamy vs non-foamy lesion differences may reflect macrophage abundance, lesion age, tissue damage, infiltrating monocytes, or broad lesion subtype.
- The snRNA signal is donor-consistent but not transcriptome-wide significant: `ACSL1` FDR `0.272`. Promotion depends on prior proteomic selection.
- Spatial MERFISH is supportive only directionally: `n=6`, non-significant pathological-vs-homeostatic contrast, borderline T-near contrast. It cannot carry a spatial validation claim.
- Effect sizes are modest and not benchmarked against generic phagocytosis, lipid droplet, interferon, hypoxia, or monocyte-infiltration markers.
- The clinical simulation assumes effect sizes without human drug-effect data.

### Biological

- `ACSL1` may simply mark lipid uptake/myelin debris processing.
- Blocking ACSL1 may reduce lipid droplets while worsening lipotoxicity, lysosomal stress, myelin-debris clearance, remyelination, or inflammatory eicosanoid diversion.
- ACSL family redundancy and compartmentalized lipid metabolism are unresolved.
- Adjacent AD/PD/AUD ACSL1 microglial studies support plausibility, not MS causality.
- Foamy microglia/macrophages may be downstream of chronic-active lesions.

### Translational

- No selective, clinically validated, CNS-engaged ACSL1 inhibitor exists in the current finding.
- Systemic ACSL1 modulation risks liver, heart, skeletal muscle, monocyte/macrophage, and repair biology toxicity.
- RNA delivery to CNS microglia remains experimental for this use case.
- PRL-positive MS is an enrichment strategy, not proof of ACSL1-high biology.
- The central missing fact is a therapeutic window.

### Novelty

- The narrow MS nomination may be novel, but foamy macrophages, lipid-droplet inflammatory myeloid states, `APOE`/`GPNMB`/`TREM2`/`SPP1` programs, and chronic lesion lipid handling are occupied fields.
- If ACSL1 is not causal or selectively actionable, novelty collapses to another marker in a known foamy myeloid program.

### Cross-Autoimmune

- Pan-autoimmune ACSL1 is unsupported without quantitative cross-disease evidence.
- ACSL1 may recur wherever monocytes/macrophages encounter damaged lipid-rich tissue; that would be pan-injury rather than pan-autoimmunity.
- CNS myelin-lipid biology may not transfer to synovium, gut, skin, kidney, salivary gland, or pancreas.

## Decisive Tests

1. Held-out MS validation with donor-level inference and adjustment for myeloid abundance, lesion class, monocyte-vs-microglia origin, and generic lipid/phagocytosis programs.
2. Spatial/protein proof in a larger paired cohort showing ACSL1 protein co-localizes with lipid-laden inflammatory microglia/macrophages at chronic-active rims and adds information beyond `GPNMB`, `APOE`, `PLIN2`, `SPP1`, `TREM2`, and myeloid density.
3. Causality gate: ACSL1 perturbation plus rescue in human myelin-debris microglia/macrophage models, preserving clearance and repair.
4. Isoform/selectivity gate: ACSL1-specific modulation or selective myeloid RNA delivery; stop if broad ACSL blockade is required.
5. Cross-autoimmune gate: at least three non-MS autoimmune datasets with donor-level ACSL1 enrichment in diseased tissue myeloid cells, ideally with non-autoimmune inflammatory controls.
6. Incremental-value test: ACSL1 must predict pathological state or injury readouts beyond the lipid-droplet myeloid module.

## Integration Verdict

Current V2 synthesis should treat ACSL1 as an overfit marker until causal and cross-disease evidence clears these gates. The defensible interim claim is: ACSL1 marks a foamy inflammatory myeloid state in MS lesions and is a candidate perturbation point. A pan-autoimmune ACSL1 therapeutic-target claim would be premature.
