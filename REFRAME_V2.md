## Alternatives Rejected For Initial Focus

### Alternative 1: Abandon ACSL1 Immediately For A Broader Pan-Autoimmune Screen

Rejected initially because ACSL1 already has unusually concrete MS lesion support in two independent modalities. A broad screen without anchoring risks rediscovering generic inflammatory genes (`IL1B`, `TNF`, `CXCL10`) that are hard to translate into a novel MS cure strategy. Broadening is necessary, but it should be constrained by a mechanistic target class.

### Alternative 2: Return To EBV-Oriented Adaptive Immunity

Rejected as the main V2 anchor because available public data still poorly connect EBV specificity to spatial lesion-rim myeloid states. EBV remains an upstream causal context, but the therapeutic-discovery phase needs an intervention point closer to chronic lesion persistence.

### Alternative 3: Promote A Known Lipid/Remyelination Node Such As `DHCR24`, `ABCA1/G1`, Or `PPARG`

Rejected initially because these have stronger prior art in MS lipid handling, making novelty harder, and because the prior ACSL1 result specifically points to long-chain fatty-acid activation rather than cholesterol efflux alone. These remain pivot candidates if ACSL1 fails.

## Initial Success Criteria

ACSL1 can remain the lead target only if it satisfies all of:

1. MS-specific evidence still reproduces under stricter checks.
2. At least four heavy backing modalities are executed and do not contradict the claim.
3. At least three autoimmune diseases outside MS show convergent ACSL1 or ACSL1-pathway evidence through independent channels.
4. A feasible intervention modality exists in principle, even if no current clinical ACSL1 drug exists.
5. The final claim avoids patient treatment recommendations and distinguishes target nomination from validated mechanism.

If any of these fail, the session must either pivot to a successor target or write `EXHAUSTION.md`.
