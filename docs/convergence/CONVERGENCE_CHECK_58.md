## Agreement Across Tracks

- `SEL1L3` is a clean underexplored marker but lacks mechanism, perturbation,
  genetics, and validated extracellular target biology.
- `FXYD5` has stronger topology and mechanism but fails direction/safety and
  remains tied to barrier/Na,K-ATPase liabilities.
- `APOC1` is a lipid-state confounder, not a tractable compartment-specific
  intervention.
- `CD82` and `LAPTM5` better resemble the endolysosomal state but are
  comparator markers, not promotable targets.

## Important Signal That Remains

The new Wave102 controller test found same-donor tissue-to-myeloid correlations
for several candidates, including `SEL1L3` links to C15/MOCCI or inflammatory
myeloid modules in Crohn disease, ulcerative colitis, psoriasis, and Sjogren
syndrome. These links do not rescue target promotion because candidate disease
residuals fail the stricter controller bar, but they do argue that the shared
mechanism may be a tissue-to-myeloid communication circuit.

## Decision

Close the accessible-marker route. The next forcing question is not "which
surface marker should be drugged?" but:

Which ligand, receptor, or pathway in tissue-resident disease compartments most
consistently predicts same-donor myeloid lipid/C15/inflammatory module state
across autoimmune tissues, and is that node druggable without blocking repair?
