# Convergence Check 13

Timestamp: 2026-05-27 08:12 UTC

## Forcing Question

After Wave31 closed direct IFN/HLA-II/CD74 transition-controller routes, can a
downstream resolution or macrophage-repair intervention supply a less blocked
therapeutic path?

## Current Evidence

- Wave32 local resolution-rescue audit did not promote any route. `TREM2/APOE`
  is parked but marker/confounder dominated. `NPC1/NPC2` has high state coupling
  but looks like a readout rather than an intervention point.
- Wave32-C prior-art/feasibility audit separates direction:
  - wrong or blocked directions: `TAM` inhibition, `GPNMB` depletion/ADC.
  - prior-art/safety blocked generic routes: `LXR/ABCA1`, `PPAR/RXR/retinoid`,
    and `TREM2` agonism as a novelty claim.
  - not blocked but insufficient: `NPC1/NPC2`, `LIPA/LAL`, `TAM` agonism,
    non-depleting `GPNMB` handle.
  - remaining whitespace: biased `FPR2`/specialized-pro-resolving mediator
    agonism and receptor-specific `CD300` modulation.

## Agreement

The strongest surviving strategic pattern is no longer "suppress the recurrent
IFN/APC state." It is "increase true resolution/efferocytosis without collapsing
host defense." Wave32-C agrees with Wave32-A/local audit that generic nuclear
lipid sensors and TREM2 are useful comparators but not novel target claims.

## Disagreement / Unresolved

- `FPR2` has the best prior-art/feasibility whitespace, but V3 does not yet have
  direct cross-autoimmune local or perturbation evidence connecting FPR2
  agonism to the lipid-lysosomal myeloid module.
- `CD300` has novelty whitespace, but receptor-specific direction is unresolved
  and no clinical-grade autoimmune modality was found.

## Next Forcing Question

Can Wave32-B or direct analysis find perturbation data showing that FPR2-biased
agonism or CD300 receptor-specific modulation increases efferocytosis/lipid
resolution in disease-relevant myeloid cells while preserving antimicrobial/IFN
host-defense markers?

If not, downstream resolution remains mechanistically attractive but not
therapeutically claim-ready.
