# Help Push An Honest MS Research Frontier

You do not need a medical background to contribute a useful research
direction. This project needs people who notice structure, failure modes, data
gaps, and decision errors that a field specialist may take for granted.

The invitation is ambitious. The evidence description is not inflated.

## The State In Sixty Seconds

Multiple sclerosis (MS) can involve inflammatory episodes called relapses and
disability that accumulates over time. The second problem, progression, is the
harder frontier this project ultimately wants to affect. `[B01-B02]`

After many rerunnable analyses, the project has:

- **one provisional monitoring research lead:** a fixed early-treatment immune
  change score with internal support in 19 participants, awaiting an
  independent test; `[M01-M04, A01]`
- **useful supported context and negative results:** several biological
  patterns recur, but none establishes a drug target; `[D01-D05]`
- **closed therapeutic routes:** real genetics did not supply the required
  causal gene, beneficial direction, and workable intervention chain;
  `[G01-G05]` and
- **a progression data wall:** no held dataset connects longitudinal molecular
  measurements to repeated confirmed disability in a design that can identify
  a progression-linked state. `[P01-P06]`

There is no validated clinical biomarker, intervention-grade target, or
established way to halt progression in this repository. That honest boundary
is where fresh ideas are useful.

## Who Could Help?

Examples include:

- **software and data engineers** who can make messy cohort packages fail
  closed rather than fail silently;
- **statisticians and causal-inference researchers** who can distinguish a
  specific signal from source, batch, time, or broad immune state;
- **control, network, and dynamical-systems thinkers** who can design
  perturbations that separate controller, feedback, and co-moving readout;
- **measurement and study-design specialists** who can turn progression into a
  timed, identifiable outcome;
- **security and privacy specialists** who can enable lawful analysis without
  exposing participant records;
- **human-factors and visualization designers** who can make uncertainty,
  abstention, and non-clinical status difficult to miss;
- **operations and access experts** who can find a lawful route to a usable
  independent cohort; and
- **skeptical generalists** who can state the strongest rival explanation and
  a fair test that could prove the project wrong.

## Six Puzzles Worth A Fresh View

1. How can a tiny-cohort monitoring rule be tested without overfitting or
   letting source/batch structure imitate validation?
2. How can an independent cohort be found or assembled when labels, timing,
   pairing, and lawful access rarely coexist?
3. What is the minimum longitudinal design that separates progression from
   relapse, static stage, and measurement noise?
4. How should a therapeutic route be designed when genetics points toward
   restoring or increasing function rather than easy inhibition?
5. What signed perturbations can distinguish a causal control point from a
   node that merely moves with a coupled system?
6. How can a future monitoring result communicate uncertainty and abstain
   without turning into treatment advice?

Read all eight [open problems](OPEN_PROBLEMS_FOR_COLLABORATORS.md) before
choosing one.

## A Useful Idea Has Five Parts

You can start with one page:

1. **Observation:** name the missing distinction before naming your favorite
   method.
2. **Prediction:** say what should differ if the idea is right and what the
   strongest rival predicts.
3. **Data:** name the independent person, donor, site, cohort, or data type and
   the required fields.
4. **Fair challenge:** include a baseline, null, holdout, confound check, or
   other test capable of weakening the idea.
5. **Drop rule:** state the result that makes the project stop or narrow the
   route.

Build this in [ten minutes](FIRST_IDEA_IN_TEN_MINUTES.md), then check the
[known non-solutions](KNOWN_NON_SOLUTIONS.md).

## What Happens To An Idea?

An idea is received as a proposal. It can be:

- ready for design freeze;
- returned for a named repair;
- waiting for a verified data field or access step;
- closed as a duplicate of a tested route;
- parked because no outcome changes a decision; or
- refused because it requests unsafe data or medical advice.

Submission, discussion, model agreement, and merge do not make an idea a
finding. Eligible data and a valid analysis determine what the project can
say. `[E01-E03]`

## Safety Boundary

Do not submit symptoms, scans, laboratory reports, medication details, genomic
records, participant rows, credentials, or any other personal/private data.
This project cannot diagnose, recommend treatment, interpret a person's
response, or provide urgent help. See [Patient And Public Safety](PATIENT_AND_PUBLIC_SAFETY.md).

## A Short Invitation You Can Share

> This public MS research project has one provisional treatment-monitoring
> lead awaiting outside validation, no intervention-grade target, and no
> established progression mechanism. It publishes its nulls and closed routes
> so new contributors can build forward. We are looking for smart generalists
> who can turn one overlooked distinction into a falsifiable test with named
> data, a rival explanation, and a drop rule. No medical background is
> required; no personal health data or treatment advice is accepted.
