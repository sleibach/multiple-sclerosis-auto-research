# Patient And Public Safety

This repository is a computational research record. It is **not medical advice,
a diagnostic service, a treatment-selection tool, or a clinical monitoring
service**. Its one live research lead has internal support but no independent
validation and no established clinical use. `[M01-M05, E01]`

## Do Not Use This Repository To Make A Care Decision

Do not use an analysis, score, gene, pathway, diagram, model response, or
predicted structure here to:

- decide whether you or another person has multiple sclerosis;
- interpret symptoms, scans, laboratory results, relapse, or disability;
- start, stop, switch, delay, or choose a medicine;
- change a dose or treatment schedule;
- predict an individual's response, relapse, or progression;
- replace a qualified clinician; or
- infer that a closed computational route rules out a person's biology.

If you need help with a personal health decision, contact a qualified health
professional who can assess the full clinical situation. If there is an
immediate health or safety concern, contact the appropriate local medical or
emergency service. This project cannot provide urgent help.

## Do Not Submit Personal Health Information

Do not put any of the following in an issue, pull request, discussion, commit,
email excerpt, screenshot, or attached file:

- names, initials, birth dates, addresses, contact details, or account IDs;
- medical-record, participant, device, or insurance identifiers;
- individual symptoms, diagnoses, medicines, scans, laboratory results, or
  disability history;
- appointment dates or location details that could identify a person;
- raw genomic, transcriptomic, imaging, or wearable data from a person;
- access credentials, consent forms, controlled-data links, or signed
  agreements; or
- any row-level clinical data unless an approved lawful process explicitly
  covers its use and storage outside public collaboration channels.

Removing a name does not necessarily make a record anonymous. Rare conditions,
dates, locations, genetics, and combined details can identify someone.

## Safe Ways To Contribute

You can contribute without sharing anyone's record:

| instead of… | submit… |
|---|---|
| “Here are my results; what should I take?” | A general research question about what data would be needed to test treatment-response monitoring safely. |
| “Does this scan prove progression?” | A method proposal for separating relapse activity, source effects, and repeated confirmed disability in an eligible cohort. |
| Uploading participant rows | A public accession, data dictionary, access tier, permitted-use summary, and aggregate sample structure. |
| Asking whether a gene should be inhibited | A direction-resolved test card stating causal-gene uncertainty, protective direction, modality, and a failure rule. |
| Sharing a private clinician message | A non-identifying statement of the general scientific distinction you think the project should test. |

Use [Contribute A Data Source](CONTRIBUTE_A_DATA_SOURCE.md) for public or
controlled-cohort metadata. Use [Your First Idea In Ten Minutes](FIRST_IDEA_IN_TEN_MINUTES.md)
for a general testable question. Neither route asks for a person's health
record.

## What The Project Can Discuss

Safe public topics include:

- the design of an analysis or validation study;
- a public dataset's documented fields and lawful access path;
- a statistical, software, visualization, or data-quality method;
- a claim's evidence status and controlling project artifact;
- a general biological mechanism framed as a future test, not advice;
- a failed route and the evidence that would genuinely reopen it; and
- documentation or accessibility improvements.

The project can explain what its code did on eligible research data. It cannot
translate that output into a recommendation for a person.

## If A Submission Crosses The Boundary

Maintainers should:

1. Avoid quoting or reproducing sensitive details.
2. Remove or restrict the content using the platform's available controls.
3. State that the repository cannot provide diagnosis or treatment advice.
4. Point the contributor to a qualified health professional for personal care.
5. Offer a privacy-safe reformulation only if the general research question can
   be separated completely from the personal record.
6. Do not analyze, download, summarize, or retain the submitted health data.

A scientifically interesting question does not justify retaining unsafe data.

## Evidence Boundary

The public onboarding layer explains existing project artifacts. It does not
validate a clinical tool. Outside sources, model suggestions, simulations, and
predicted structures cannot supply personal medical advice or upgrade a project
finding. Missing project data also cannot rule out biology in an individual.
`[E01-E03, P01-P02]`
