# Newcomer Comprehension Test Kit

This kit tests whether the onboarding creates the intended mental model in a
smart reader without medical training. It does **not** test scientific truth,
clinical knowledge, intelligence, or suitability for medical decisions. It
collects no health information.

The project already ran a model-based reading audit as an editorial aid. That
cannot substitute for real people: models have different reading behavior and
their answers are not evidence. `[E03]`

## What The Test Should Reveal

A successful reader should be able to state, in their own words:

1. the project has one provisional monitoring lead;
2. monitoring is not treatment selection, a target, or a cure;
3. independent validation is still missing;
4. several attractive routes are closed or bounded for specific reasons;
5. progression remains unanswered because the required longitudinal design is
   missing;
6. a data boundary is not proof that biology is absent;
7. outside sources and models can suggest questions but do not become project
   evidence; and
8. a useful contribution names a prediction, data, error control, and drop
   condition.

These are comprehension targets derived from existing project boundaries, not
new findings. `[M01, M05, A01, P01-P03, E01-E03]`

## Participant And Privacy Boundaries

### Intended participant

- An adult volunteer comfortable reading technical English.
- Medical training is not required; ideally the person has not already read
  this repository.
- Engineers, designers, data workers, scientists from other fields, and systems
  thinkers are useful audiences.

### Do not collect

- diagnosis, symptoms, disability, medication, family history, or other health
  information;
- employer-sensitive, protected, or confidential data;
- a real patient scenario; or
- identifying information beyond an optional study code.

This kit is intended for documentation usability, not biomedical inference or
medical advice. Local rules may still classify structured testing with people
as human-subject research. Obtain the appropriate ethics and privacy review
before formal study, publication, or collection beyond ordinary documentation
feedback.

## Choose One Route Before Testing

### Route A: Two-Minute Orientation

Give the participant only:

1. [the two-minute version](MS_RESEARCH_EXPLAINED.md#the-two-minute-version);
2. the [research map](visuals/RESEARCH_MAP_V55.svg); and
3. no glossary unless they choose to open it.

Allow up to five minutes. Ask questions Q01-Q06 below. This tests whether the
fast route prevents dangerous overreads; it is not expected to transmit the
entire project.

### Route B: Fifteen-Minute Contributor Readiness

Give the participant:

1. [the fifteen-minute version](MS_RESEARCH_EXPLAINED.md#the-fifteen-minute-version);
2. the [visual guide](VISUAL_INDEX.md);
3. the [open-problem board](OPEN_PROBLEMS_FOR_COLLABORATORS.md); and
4. the [contribution on-ramp](HOW_TO_CONTRIBUTE_IDEAS.md).

Allow 15-25 minutes. Ask Q01-Q12. The reader may use the glossary but should not
search the wider repository during the first answer pass.

Record which route and pages were actually used. Do not combine results from
the two routes as if exposure were identical.

## Facilitator Script

Read this without adding scientific hints:

> We are testing the documentation, not you. Please read the supplied pages as
> you normally would. Afterwards, explain what you think the project knows,
> does not know, and needs next. It is useful if something is confusing. Please
> do not share personal health information. I will not correct answers until
> the first pass is complete.

During the first pass:

- ask each question exactly as written;
- do not praise, correct, or lead;
- ask “What in the material led you there?” only after the initial answer;
- record uncertainty and navigation difficulty as useful observations; and
- do not turn the exercise into a medical discussion.

After the first pass, correct any treatment or medical-advice misunderstanding
immediately and show the relevant source-linked boundary.

## Questions

### Q01 · What is the one live clinical lead?

Ask: **What is the strongest live result, and what kind of thing is it?**

Expected concept: a provisional early-treatment APC/HLA-II-related monitoring
score awaiting independent validation. It is not yet a validated biomarker or
clinical tool. `[M01]`

Critical overread: calling it a treatment recommendation, established clinical
test, target, cure, or progression measure.

### Q02 · What does “monitoring, not target” mean?

Ask: **What could the score report, and what can it not establish?**

Expected concept: it may report a treatment-associated biological change; it
does not identify what to drug, prove efficacy, or choose therapy. `[M05]`

Critical overread: using the score to recommend starting, stopping, or changing
treatment.

### Q03 · What evidence is still missing?

Ask: **What must happen before confidence materially increases?**

Expected concept: an unchanged, preregistered analysis on a correctly
structured independent cohort, with pass/fail/inconclusive interpretations
fixed before outcomes are seen. `[A01]`

Critical overread: repeated internal analysis or post-hoc threshold changes
count as external validation.

### Q04 · What limits the monitoring interpretation?

Ask: **Did the confounder work prove a pure APC/HLA-II mechanism?**

Expected concept: no. Tested steroid-response and simple composition proxies
did not explain the score, but broad immune tone attenuated it; the small sample
and proxy measurements bound interpretation. `[M04]`

Critical overread: claiming direct steroid exposure was measured or that the
score is mechanism-specific.

### Q05 · What did the project establish about progression?

Ask: **Did it find a progression biomarker, mechanism, or way to halt
progression? Why or why not?**

Expected concept: no. The held corpus lacks repeated molecular measurements
linked to repeated confirmed-disability outcomes in the same people, and no
progression target or treatment effect was established. `[P01-P05]`

Critical overread: “the data show no progression biology exists.”

### Q06 · Name one closed or bounded route and why it is not open.

Expected examples include causal-gene/direction problems at KIF21B/GPR25,
conflicting PTGER4 signals/directions, no gain from coupled-axis model
complexity, or an unsupported patient simulator. `[G03-G05, D02-D03]`

Critical overread: protein class, a predicted pocket, or another model is
enough to reopen a route.

### Q07 · What is the status of ZMIZ1?

Ask: **Is it a closed biological result, a target, or something else?**

Expected concept: a supported decoupling warning. The cross-disease directions
differ, so simple target transfer is unsafe; this neither nominates a target nor
proves irrelevance. `[G02]`

Critical overread: grouping ZMIZ1 with closed target routes without preserving
the supported decoupling result.

### Q08 · What was the brain-bank/source lesson?

Expected concept: source and diagnosis can align so strongly that source can
mimic disease association. This bounded one microglia analysis; it does not
invalidate every brain-bank study. `[C01-C02]`

Critical overread: adjustment proves causality, or all brain-bank data are
invalid.

### Q09 · What does “discovery exhausted” mean here?

Expected concept: the same assembled corpus and tested joint gate yielded no
unexpected held-out-validated signal, making genuinely new data preferable to
more unconstrained mining. It is not a universal limit on biology or future
methods. `[D04-D05]`

Critical overread: the reported 0.127 is an MS effect-size ceiling or proof no
public dataset can ever help.

### Q10 · What authority do literature, AlphaFold, and AI models have?

Expected concept: they are separately labeled context or proposal sources.
They can motivate tests but do not become rerunnable project evidence merely by
being cited, predicted, or agreed upon. `[E02-E03]`

Critical overread: a paper, prediction, or model consensus overrides a grounded
result.

### Q11 · What would make an outsider idea useful?

Expected concept: a current boundary, changed observable prediction, reachable
data, comparator/null, holdout or correction, confounder plan, decision
consequence, and a result that drops or narrows the idea.

Critical overread: novelty or plausibility alone earns finding status.

### Q12 · Where would you start contributing?

Expected concept: the participant can choose an open problem or role route,
identify the nearest closed route, and find the research-direction template.

This is a navigation check. There is no single correct scientific direction.

## Scoring Rubric

Score the first answer, before correction:

| grade | rule |
|---|---|
| `2 · clear` | Preserves the expected distinction and scope in the participant's own words. |
| `1 · partial` | Core direction is right but an important scope limit is missing or vague. |
| `0 · absent` | Cannot answer or gives unrelated material without a dangerous claim. |
| `-1 · dangerous overread` | Converts provisional evidence into treatment advice, target/cure, progression result, absence-of-biology claim, or ungrounded authority. |

Do not award points for repeating exact terminology without explaining the
distinction.

### Route-level interpretation

**Route A succeeds provisionally as onboarding** when Q01-Q06 total at least 9
of 12, Q01/Q02/Q05 are each at least partial, and no answer receives `-1`.

**Route B succeeds provisionally as contributor onboarding** when the total is
at least 19 of 24, Q01/Q02/Q05/Q10/Q11 are each at least partial, and no answer
receives `-1`.

These thresholds are usability decisions fixed for this kit. They do not grade
the participant and do not validate a project finding. Report item-level
patterns; a single total can hide a serious misconception.

Use the [TSV scorecard template](templates/NEWCOMER_COMPREHENSION_SCORECARD_V55.tsv).

## What To Do With A Failure

| failure pattern | documentation response |
|---|---|
| Monitoring becomes treatment selection | Strengthen the monitor/target block where the participant read it; test again with a new reader. |
| Missing progression data becomes absent biology | Put the required design and “not evidence of absence” caveat closer to the first progression statement. |
| ZMIZ1 becomes a closed target | Separate supported decoupling from target-route closure visually and verbally. |
| Outside context becomes evidence | Strengthen the evidence-lanes explanation and source labels. |
| Reader cannot name a contribution route | Shorten navigation and link directly from the page where they stopped. |
| Vocabulary blocks an otherwise correct answer | Add or improve a glossary definition without deleting the evidence boundary. |

Do not change a scientific claim to improve a comprehension score. Change
wording, ordering, navigation, or examples; then rerun source and integrity
checks.
