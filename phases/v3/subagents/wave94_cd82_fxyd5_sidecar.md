Date: 2026-05-27

Role: sidecar audit after lipid/GPR183 closures. This note evaluates whether `CD82` or `FXYD5` should remain as underexplored accessible cell-state transition handles, and whether either deserves a bounded forcing test. This is not a V3 finding claim.

## Prior-Art Readout

### CD82

Local prior closure already says `CD82` lacks a clean intervention direction or mature autoimmune-ready modality, and belongs to the broad-expression/no-strict-core-residual group (`subagents_v3/wave21_residual_candidate_prior_art.md:42-54`). Wave39 then carried those prior flags forward (`results_v3/wave39_surfaceome_rescue_after_resolution_pivot/surfaceome_rescue_rank.tsv:221`), and Wave40 converted the row to fail-fast no-go (`results_v3/wave40_parked_surface_failfast/REPORT.md:11`).

External prior art does not show a clinical autoimmune CD82 intervention blocker, but it does remove the simplest novelty story. The RA synovial fibroblast literature reports CD82 upregulation in RA synovial fibroblasts and shows that CD82 perturbation changes migration, adhesion, RASF-endothelial interaction, and invasion-site localization (ScienceDirect/Ovid entry for doi `10.1136/annrheumdis-2018-212954`). The direction is not clean: increasing CD82 can reduce migration and adhesion, while reduced CD82 can increase migration/matrix adhesion, and the conclusion frames CD82 as helping keep aggressive RASF at inflammatory/tissue-damage sites.

Mechanistically, CD82 is also a tetraspanin platform rather than a single linear receptor. A macrophage study reports CD82 as a regulator of TLR9 trafficking/signaling and TLR9-dependent NF-kappaB cytokine activation, while also noting broad tetraspanin roles in migration, adhesion, protein trafficking, and signal transduction (PMC `CD82 controls CpG-dependent TLR9 signaling`: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6988855/). Demyelinating-disease autoantibody work does not support CD82 as a pathogenic antibody antigen or biomarker; it found only weak/unclear tetraspanin reactivity and concluded these proteins are unlikely antibody targets in demyelinating disease (ScienceDirect entry `Autoantibodies to tetraspanins (CD9, CD81 and CD82) in demyelinating diseases`: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165572815301065).

Prior-art conclusion for `CD82`: no direct autoimmune clinical intervention saturation, but enough RA/function and innate-immune biology exists that a generic "CD82 as accessible autoimmune target" would be weak. The hard blocker is targetability/direction, not novelty.

### FXYD5

Local prior closure is stricter after Wave51: `FXYD5` remains reachable-looking but not promotable because it lacks a clear modality, FDR-supported MS anchor, target-level genetics, strict residual state survival, perturbation evidence, and direction/safety resolution (`results_v3/wave51_reachable_stromal_surface_audit/REPORT.md:9-10`, `results_v3/wave51_reachable_stromal_surface_audit/REPORT.md:22-29`). It passes only the novelty-not-blocking gate in Wave51 (`results_v3/wave51_reachable_stromal_surface_audit/REPORT.md:29`), and the public API table found 0 exact autoimmune hits and 0 autoimmune clinical trials in the targeted queries (`results_v3/wave51_reachable_stromal_surface_audit/public_api_counts.tsv:5-7`).

External prior art supports the direction/safety blocker. Reviews and mechanistic work describe FXYD5/dysadherin as a Na,K-ATPase regulator affecting cell-cell junctions, adhesion, polarity, chemokine production, and cancer progression (PubMed `27066483`; PMC FXYD5 adhesion paper: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4920254/). Oncology modality precedent exists: EDC1/DYS-ADC uses an anti-dysadherin antibody with a cardiac-glycoside/Na,K-ATPase-directed payload and is described as preclinical oncology (`https://www.adcreview.com/drugmap/edc1/`). The corresponding extracellular targeted drug-conjugate patent explicitly names FXYD5/dysadherin as the extracellular target and links the antibody conjugate to cardiac glycosides and Na,K-ATPase activity (`EP2475391B1`: https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/44/7b/ba/41285c567683cf/EP2475391B1.pdf).

Prior-art conclusion for `FXYD5`: not blocked by direct autoimmune clinical prior art, but blocked from promotion by modality mismatch and safety. A cytotoxic FXYD5/Na,K-ATPase route is not an acceptable autoimmune transition-handle strategy; only a non-depleting, barrier-preserving perturbation could be informative.

## Forcing-Test Specification

`CD82` does not deserve a standalone forcing test. If an existing ex vivo stromal/fibroblast or epithelial transition assay already includes tetraspanin perturbations, `CD82` can be bundled as a negative/control arm. Reopen criteria would require all of the following: isoform/complex-specific CD82 target engagement, directionally interpretable agonism or blockade, disease-state reversal that outperforms generic IFN/NF-kappaB suppression, no increase in invasive migration or harmful retention, and independent strict residual or target-resolved support. Current evidence does not justify building that assay only for CD82.

`FXYD5` deserves one bounded falsification test, because it is the only one of the pair that Wave40 left as `PARK_ONLY_IF_NEW_PERTURBATION` and because direct autoimmune intervention prior art is not saturated. The test should be framed as a kill test, not a promotion test:

- Context: human UC epithelial organoid/slice or inflamed epithelial-stromal/endothelial co-culture, with optional psoriasis keratinocyte or T1D endothelial follow-up only if the primary context passes.
- Perturbation: non-depleting anti-FXYD5 blocking/engagement reagent, CRISPRi/siRNA, or controlled overexpression/ectodomain perturbation. Do not use FXYD5-targeted cardiac-glycoside/cytotoxic EDC logic.
- Required readouts: disease-state module reversal, E-cadherin/tight-junction/barrier preservation, Na,K-ATPase activity, epithelial viability, inflammatory NF-kappaB/IFN/HLA module specificity, and no worsening of migration/invasion or repair markers.
- Pass condition: replicated reversal of the FXYD5-positive pathogenic compartment with preserved or improved barrier/repair function and target engagement, plus an orthogonal perturbation in the same direction.
- Kill condition: any barrier impairment, generic toxicity, generic anti-inflammatory suppression without FXYD5-specific target engagement, reproduction of the Crohn-negative direction conflict, or no replicated disease-state reversal.

## Final Call

Neither `CD82` nor `FXYD5` is target-ready or intervention-grade.

`CD82` should remain closed as an accessible tetraspanin/state-marker control.

`FXYD5` should receive, at most, a single narrowly specified forcing test. If no non-depleting, barrier-preserving perturbation assay is available, it should remain parked rather than promoted.
