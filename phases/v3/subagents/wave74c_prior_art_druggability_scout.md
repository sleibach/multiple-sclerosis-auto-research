## EPHX2 / sEH

Closest prior art:

- Patent: [WO2000023060A2](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2000023060A2/en), "Method of treating immunological disorders mediated by t-lymphocytes", claims soluble epoxide hydrolase inhibitors for immunological/autoimmune disorders and explicitly lists RA, Sjogren's syndrome, diabetes mellitus, MS, psoriasis, Crohn's disease, and UC.
- Patent: [WO2022200105A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2022200105A1/en), "Compounds as soluble epoxide hydrolase inhibitors", lists inflammation, immunological disorders, arthritis, IBD, colitis, MS, CNS injury/neurodegeneration, diabetes/metabolic disease.
- Literature: [TPPU ameliorates EAE/MS model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8125305/) and [EPHX2 inhibition preclinical IBD evaluation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6474586/) put the target directly into MS and IBD disease models.
- Clinical: sEH inhibitors have human trials, but not direct autoimmune/MS/IBD efficacy trials located in this pass: EC5026 Phase 1 healthy/pain ([NCT04228302](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04228302), [NCT04908995](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04908995)), Parkinson's ([NCT07142044](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07142044)), SAH ([NCT03318783](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03318783)), hypertension/glucose metabolism ([NCT00847899](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00847899)).

Druggability/tissue penetration:

- Chemical matter is not the bottleneck. EC5026 is described as picomolar, orally active, and selective in a peer-reviewed development paper ([PMC7917437](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7917437/)). TPPU has literature support for CNS exposure/BBB-relevant pharmacology, including brain/BBB injury models.
- The novelty problem is severe: broad use claims plus disease-model publications already cover the obvious autoimmune/MS/IBD story. A delta would need to be narrower than "inhibit EPHX2 for autoimmune inflammation", e.g., a genotype/oxylipin-stratified subpopulation, a non-catalytic EPHX2 modality, local delivery, or a defined combination not taught by the older claims.

## GPR183 / EBI2

Closest prior art:

- Clinical: IPG11406 is listed as an oral GPR183 antagonist in UC Phase 2, [NCT07535489](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07535489), estimated start 2026-07-07, with clinical remission at week 12 as the main efficacy endpoint. IPG11406 is also listed in lupus nephritis Phase IIa, [NCT06717815](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06717815), and Phase 1 healthy-volunteer testing, [NCT06255834](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06255834).
- Medicinal chemistry: "Discovery of a First-in-Class GPR183 Antagonist for the Potential Treatment of Rheumatoid Arthritis" reports compound 32/IPG11406 as potent/selective with improved solubility, mitigated hERG, excellent PK, and CIA mouse efficacy ([ACS J. Med. Chem. abstract](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acs.jmedchem.3c01364); PubMed PMID [38047891](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38047891/)).
- Patent: [WO2024208303A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2024208303A1/en), "Compounds and their uses as GPR183 inhibitors", explicitly links the GPR183-oxysterol system to IBD and claims GPR183-mediated disease including autoimmune diseases.
- Patent: [US11919895B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11919895B2/en), GPR183 antagonists for pain, includes autoimmune neuropathy language; not the cleanest autoimmune blocker but demonstrates active GPR183 antagonist IP space.
- Biology: GPR183/EBI2 has direct autoimmune/neuroinflammation literature: high expression in MS lesions and promotion of early CNS migration of encephalitogenic CD4 T cells ([Cell Reports page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211124717300578)); IBD linkage and immune-cell positioning are discussed in structural/review literature ([PubMed 35537452](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35537452/), [PMC4371701](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4371701/)).

Druggability/tissue penetration:

- Selective small-molecule antagonism is plausible enough to be clinically tested. Earlier NIBR189/GSK/NIBR ligands had ADMET/pharmacology liabilities, but compound 32/IPG11406 appears designed to solve solubility/hERG/PK liabilities.
- CNS penetration remains less clearly established for IPG11406/GPR183 antagonists than for P2RX7 or sEH inhibitors. Because GPR183 is expressed by infiltrating immune cells and barrier/tissue compartments, an MS claim would still need CNS exposure or immune-trafficking PD evidence, not just peripheral potency.
- Deltas left: possibly MS-specific CNS trafficking, tissue-resident macrophage/DC positioning, or an orthogonal chemotype; however UC/LN clinical entry and RA/IBD patent/literature make broad autoimmune/IBD/RA novelty blocked.

## P2RX7 / P2X7

Closest prior art:

- Clinical RA: Pfizer CE-224,535 P2X7 antagonist was tested in methotrexate-inadequate RA, [NCT00628095](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00628095). The associated publication is "Efficacy and safety of CE-224,535..." ([PubMed 22382341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22382341/)).
- Clinical Crohn's: AZD9056 Phase IIa in moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease showed tolerability and symptom signal but not a clean disease-modifying blocker-release result; see [PubMed 26197451](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26197451/).
- Clinical RA AZD9056: phase IIa/IIb RA evaluation is summarized as no clinically meaningful effect in the six-month Phase IIb study ([Ann Rheum Dis/Ovid page](https://www.ovid.com/journals/ardi/fulltext/10.1136/annrheumdis-2011-143578~clinical-evaluation-of-the-efficacy-of-the-p2x7-purinergic); review summary [PMC3915485](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3915485/)).
- Patent: P2X7 antagonist method/compound claims broadly cover inflammatory and autoimmune indications, e.g. [WO2004099146A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2004099146), and MS-specific use of P2X7 antagonists o-ATP/BBG is claimed in [EP1655032B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1655032B1/en).
- Literature: P2X7 is extensively reviewed in autoimmunity and MS/neuroinflammation ([PMC10531565](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10531565/), [PubMed 31015145](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31015145/)); EAE genetic/pharmacology literature exists ([Journal of Neuroinflammation 2008](https://jneuroinflammation.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1742-2094-5-33)).

Druggability/tissue penetration:

- Chemical selectivity is not the blocker. CE-224,535 was optimized as a selective P2X7 clinical candidate ([PubMed 21565499](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21565499/)). Brain-penetrant selective P2X7 antagonists are also documented, especially JNJ-54175446, with human CSF/brain-penetration and PD evidence ([PubMed 30260294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30260294/); [Translational Psychiatry 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-023-02557-5)).
- The hostile issue is translation: human autoimmune trials did not convert strong inflammasome/IL-1 biology into convincing RA efficacy, and Crohn's evidence is not enough to support novelty. A viable delta would require a biomarker-defined P2RX7 gain-of-function subgroup, CNS PET occupancy/target-engagement in active MS lesions, or a local gut/CNS strategy that avoids replaying AZD9056/CE-224,535.

## Bottom line

None of the three is clean for broad autoimmune/MS/IBD novelty. EPHX2 and GPR183 are directly blocked by prior-art/clinical-program overlap. P2RX7 is the most useful comparator because it shows the classic trap: selective and even CNS-penetrant matter exists, but RA/Crohn's clinical translation has already been tried and is not a clean success signal.
