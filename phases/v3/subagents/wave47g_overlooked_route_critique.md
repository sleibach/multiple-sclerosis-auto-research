# Wave47-G Overlooked-Route Critique

Date: 2026-05-27

Role: hostile late-stage overlooked-route critic after Wave46 central-axis closure.

Scope: local V3 artifacts only, with emphasis on `LAB_NOTEBOOK_V3.md`,
`ORCHESTRATION_LOG_V3.md`, Wave32, Wave33, Wave34, Wave38, and Wave46 outputs.
The question was whether "all direct module routes are closed" missed a
therapeutic route that is not a relabel of IFN/JAK, CD74/HLA-II, CIITA/RFX5,
IFI30/GILT, CTSS, ACSL1, FADS, CFB, NAMPT, or generic tolerance/costimulation.

## Bottom Line

No route earns `PROMOTE_CANDIDATE`.

The closure claim survives if interpreted as **direct module-target promotion is
closed for V3**. The only nontrivial overlooked therapeutic route is not a final
target: biased `FPR2/ANXA1` pro-resolution agonism, with receptor-specific
`CD300F/CD300A` modulation as a weaker sibling. Both are outside the direct
IFN/HLA-II/lysosomal target set, but both were already surfaced by Wave32 and
then left parked because they lack the required MS anchor, target-level genetics
or target-specific perturbation, and mature safety/direction package.

Strict verdict:

- `FPR2/ANXA1` biased pro-resolution agonism: `REOPEN_WITH_NEW_TEST`.
- `CD300F/CD300A` receptor-specific apoptotic-lipid/efferocytosis tuning:
  `REOPEN_WITH_NEW_TEST`, weaker than FPR2 and only if receptor-specific.
- All other reviewed orthogonal-looking branches: `NO_GO`.

This is not evidence that Wave46 missed a promotable direct route. It is evidence
that one or two **future assay branches** remain, both downstream/orthogonal and
not ready for a therapeutic claim.

## The Only Real Reopen: `FPR2/ANXA1`

I would not call this a direct module route. It is a downstream resolution
pharmacology route that tries to alter macrophage handling of inflammatory cargo
without suppressing the IFN/HLA-II core.

Why it is not dead:

- It is not a relabel of IFN/JAK, CD74/HLA-II, CIITA/RFX5, IFI30/GILT, CTSS,
  ACSL1/FADS/CFB/NAMPT, or generic tolerance.
- Wave32-A identified it as the main branch not fully exhausted by automated
  module-first screens.
- Wave32-C judged it "not blocked, but immature" rather than novelty-blocked.
- It has a plausible druggability lane: biased GPCR agonism, lipid mediator
  analogs, or ANXA1-like agonist biology.

Why it cannot be promoted:

- The local V3 signal is IBD-skewed. Wave32-A notes Crohn/UC myeloid `FPR2`
  positivity but weak/non-supportive MS white-matter evidence.
- No target-level autoimmune genetics package is present.
- Wave37 direct efferocytosis CRISPR showed `FPR2` as unresolved, not a clean
  dependency.
- Wave36-B explicitly required FPR2 blockade/knockdown dependency plus human
  disease tissue rescue before reopening this as more than a comparator.
- Prior-art is not empty: resolution pharmacology and FPR2 agonism are active
  fields, so novelty would need ligand bias, disease compartment, and biomarker
  differentiation.

Minimum new test to reopen:

1. Human Crohn/UC or LN macrophage/slice assay plus MS myelin-loaded human
   microglia/macrophage assay.
2. Biased FPR2 agonist or ANXA1 mimetic panel with dose response.
3. On-target dependency by FPR2 blockade or knockdown.
4. Functional cargo readout: apoptotic-cell or myelin/lipid-debris clearance.
5. Required molecular result: reduce lipid-inflammatory/APC state without
   collapsing antiviral IFN response or antigen-presentation competence.
6. Guardrails: no fibrosis/TGFB stress program, no neutrophil-like inflammatory
   ligand-bias signature.

Until that exists, this is a **wet-lab branch**, not a candidate.

## The Weaker Reopen: `CD300F/CD300A`

This route survives only as a receptor-specific perturbation gap. It should not
be written as "CD300 modulation" because the family contains receptors with
opposite or context-dependent effects.

Why it is not dead:

- It is orthogonal to the central IFN/HLA-II/lysosomal route.
- It is not already saturated at the clinical autoimmune-modality level in the
  local Wave32-C search.
- A receptor-specific agonist/inhibitory-tuning experiment could be new evidence
  rather than a narrative reframing.

Why it cannot be promoted:

- Wave32 automated gates call `CD300_RESOLUTION_CHECKPOINT` no-go: no state
  coupling, no genetic/real perturbation anchor, no independent validation.
- Wave32-A explicitly demotes the family as direction-ambiguous.
- No clean autoimmune CD300 perturbation dataset was found in the V3 search.
- Wave37 tracked `CD300A` and `CD300LF` as unresolved in the efferocytosis CRISPR
  screen, not rescued dependencies.

Minimum new test to reopen:

- Receptor-specific perturbation in primary human macrophages/microglia, not a
  family-level antibody.
- Separate `CD300F`, `CD300A`, `CD300LF`, and `CD300E`.
- Show improved efferocytosis/myelin-lipid cargo handling and reduced pathogenic
  inflammatory/lipid-APC state.
- Preserve antigen presentation and host defense.
- Demonstrate disease-compartment recurrence in MS plus at least two peripheral
  autoimmune tissues, or provide target-level genetics/coloc.

## Why Other Tempting Routes Stay Closed

Genetics-first candidates mostly fail the same way: broad mapped-gene recurrence
does not equal target-level causality. Wave34 explicitly warns that its GWAS
Catalog overlaps are locus-level evidence only. `PTPN2`, `PTPN22`, `TNFAIP3`,
`SH2B3`, `CLEC16A`, `IRF5`, `GPR65`, `FAP`, and `CCR6` all need either
target-resolved coloc/MR or real disease-tissue perturbation before they can
override local state and modality failures.

Perturbation-first candidates also fail. Wave36-A found rescue-like submodules,
but no route replicated across datasets with clean direction, stress/fibrosis
guardrails, and feasible modality. Wave38 is the harshest closure: direct
efferocytosis CRISPR hits did not promote after adding disease-state,
druggability, MS anchor, and prior-art gates.

Accessibility-first candidates fail because accessibility is not direction.
Wave39-B's exclusions are correct: state-definition markers, antigen-presentation
machinery, cathepsins/lysosomal loading, complement/Fc uptake, TAM/TREM repair,
crowded checkpoint/glycan/adhesion axes, and secreted injury markers are not
therapeutic routes without new causal perturbation evidence.

## Final Call

The statement "all direct module routes are closed" is defensible.

I would refine it to:

> All **direct target-nomination routes from the V3 module evidence** are closed.
> The only remaining non-relabel branches are assay-level reopeners:
> biased `FPR2/ANXA1` pro-resolution agonism, and weaker receptor-specific
> `CD300F/CD300A` tuning. Neither is promotable without new target-specific
> human perturbation evidence.
