# Wave75-C ETS2 prior-art and directionality scout

Date: 2026-05-27. Scope: hostile prior-art and intervention-directionality scan
for the `ETS2` inflammatory macrophage axis and nearby intervention routes:
direct `ETS2`, upstream MEK/ERK, chr21q22/gene-desert enhancer biology, and
AP-1/ETS macrophage programs. This is not a therapeutic finding.

## Call

`PARK_NARROW_DELTA_ONLY`

The broad thesis is already published: a chr21q22 disease-associated gene
desert contains a macrophage enhancer variant that increases `ETS2`, `ETS2` is
reported as necessary and sufficient for inflammatory macrophage functions, and
the paper frames the axis as a shared inflammatory disease mechanism with
therapeutic relevance. Direct `ETS2` remains hard to drug; upstream MEK/ERK can
reduce `ETS2` activation/signature but is broad, oncology-precedented, toxic,
and already clinically tested in RA. A defensible delta would need to be much
narrower than "inhibit ETS2/MEK for autoimmune macrophage inflammation".

## Closest Prior Art

The anchor paper is already the key blocker. It maps a chr21q22 haplotype and
the lead variant `rs2836882`, shows long-range interaction with the `ETS2`
promoter in macrophages, reports allele-dependent enhancer activity, and places
`ETS2` upstream of a pro-inflammatory macrophage state. It explicitly states
that `ETS2` knockdown suppresses inflammatory cytokines and that overexpression
recapitulates inflammatory macrophage functions. It also reports that
macrophage `ETS2` expression is elevated by disease-associated genetic variants
in IBD, ankylosing spondylitis, primary sclerosing cholangitis, and Takayasu's
arteritis.

The same paper tests a translational route: MEK inhibition reduced `ETS2`
phosphorylation and inhibited the `ETS2` signature in macrophages and ex vivo
Crohn's/UC biopsy cultures. That makes "MEK upstream of ETS2 for IBD
macrophage inflammation" explicit prior art, even before considering older MEK
clinical/toxicity baggage. The paper also states the authors filed a patent
application related to the work; I did not locate a public Google Patents family
with the exact ETS2/macrophage wording in this pass.

Europe PMC/PubMed follow-on results show rapid literature uptake:

- Europe PMC query `ETS2 macrophage inflammatory bowel disease`: hitCount 131,
  including an IBD narrative review, a UC/M1 macrophage paper, and the primary
  Nature paper.
- PubMed query `ETS2[Title/Abstract] AND macrophage[Title/Abstract] AND

- Europe PMC query `ETS2 rs2836882 inflammatory bowel disease ankylosing

## Direct ETS2 Druggability

Direct `ETS2` modulation is the weakest intervention route. `ETS2` is a nuclear
transcription factor, and the anchor paper itself frames direct inhibitors as
not currently available, while suggesting newer approaches such as proteolysis
targeting chimeras or macrophage-targeted oligonucleotide delivery. ChEMBL API
query `target/search.json?q=ETS2` returned `targets 0` in this pass, so I did
not find a curated small-molecule target record or clinical-grade direct ETS2
matter.

Possible direct routes are therefore not conventional "drug the active site":

- macrophage-targeted siRNA/ASO to reduce `ETS2`;
- degrader/glue strategy if an ETS2 binder or recruited interface can be found;
- CRISPRi/epigenome editing of the disease enhancer, not realistic as a near
  therapeutic route;
- ligand-directed nanoparticle or local gut delivery to avoid systemic ETS2
  suppression.

Hostile read: these routes are plausible platform ideas, not established
target tractability. They also overlap the primary paper's own suggested
future-therapy language, so novelty would have to sit in delivery, selectivity,
subpopulation, or disease-local validation.

## MEK/ERK Upstream Route

MEK/ERK is pharmacologically tractable but unattractive for a new autoimmune
finding:

- The primary Nature paper already uses MEK inhibition as the practical way to
  suppress `ETS2` activation/signature in macrophage and IBD tissue systems.
- ClinicalTrials.gov query `(ARRY-438162 OR binimetinib OR MEK162) rheumatoid`
  returned [NCT00650767](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00650767), a
  completed RA trial of ARRY-438162.
- MEK inhibitors are oncology drugs with recognized broad pathway toxicities;
  ClinicalTrials.gov now even lists a neuromuscular-complication case-series
  study for MEK inhibitors, [NCT06740656](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06740656).
- Systemic MEK/ERK inhibition would hit many immune and non-immune AP-1/ETS
  programs, not just inflammatory macrophages.

Hostile read: MEK/ERK is too prior-arted, too broad, and too toxic for the
desired narrow inflammatory macrophage axis. It can be used as a perturbation
tool or positive control, but not as a clean therapeutic novelty route.

## Directionality

The direction from the anchor paper is coherent for the genetically covered
diseases: disease risk increases macrophage `ETS2` expression/activity, and
reducing `ETS2` or its MEK-dependent activation suppresses inflammatory
macrophage outputs. That makes the direction "lower ETS2 activity in disease
macrophages" plausible for IBD/AS/PSC/Takayasu.

The direction is not automatically portable to RA/MS/psoriasis. Those diseases
need local evidence that the pathogenic compartment is an `ETS2`-high
macrophage/microglia or monocyte-derived macrophage program, not only generic
IFN/AP-1/MAPK inflammation, tissue injury, or infiltrating myeloid abundance.
For MS specifically, CNS macrophage/microglia state, lesion stage, and
peripheral immune effects could easily create wrong-compartment or
wrong-timing risk.

## Plausible Narrow Deltas

Only narrow, falsifiable deltas survive this scan:

1. MS-local support: show `ETS2` activity is enriched in active MS lesion
   macrophage/microglia states and remains positive after residualizing against
   generic IFN, HLA-II/APC, AP-1 stress, and cell-mixture signals.
2. Delivery delta: macrophage-selective or gut-local `ETS2` suppression that
   avoids systemic MEK/ERK inhibition and avoids broad nuclear TF suppression.
3. Genotype delta: restrict to `rs2836882`/chr21q22 risk-haplotype carriers or
   an enhancer-activity biomarker, with disease-local PD readout.
4. Modality delta: an ETS2-selective degrader/glue/oligonucleotide with real
   macrophage selectivity, not generic MEK inhibition.
5. Combination delta: pair very low-dose ETS2-axis suppression with a
   macrophage-state-specific agent only if local data show additive reversal
   beyond MEK/AP-1/IFN generic suppression.

## Falsifiers Before Reopening

Close permanently if any of the following occur:

- `ETS2` is not positive in MS lesion macrophage/microglia after IFN/APC/AP-1
  residualization.
- The cross-disease signal is only IBD/AS and does not reproduce in RA/MS/
  psoriasis local datasets.
- MEK inhibitors are the only available perturbation that reverses the module.
- ETS2 knockdown/degradation suppresses viability or generic macrophage
  activation more than a disease-specific inflammatory program.
- Patent search later reveals public claims covering ETS2 inhibition/degradation
  or macrophage-targeted ETS2 oligonucleotides for autoimmune/inflammatory
  disease.

## Bottom Line

Do not claim ETS2 as a new cross-autoimmune target. The broad macrophage
inflammatory axis and its IBD/AS/PSC/Takayasu directionality are already
published, and the upstream MEK route is explicitly tested and unattractive as a
therapeutic route. Park this unless local data can support a narrow MS- or
RA/psoriasis-specific, macrophage-local, non-MEK, non-generic intervention
delta.
