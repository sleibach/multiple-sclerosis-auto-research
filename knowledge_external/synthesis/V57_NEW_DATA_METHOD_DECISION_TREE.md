## Why Route 1 Is Different

- **[external-unverifiable; source: `knowledge_external/records/method_human_ipsc_microglia_crispri_a_2022.json`]** Human iPSC-derived microglia have been used with
  inducible CRISPRi and CRISPRa plus single-cell readouts.
- **[grounded]** The project repeatedly encountered wrong-direction or
  restoration-direction target constraints and context-dependent single-factor
  failures. Sources: `docs/history/FAILURE_STRUCTURE_AND_EXCLUSION_V39.md` and
  `docs/reports/THERAPEUTIC_PATH_V52.md`.
- **[method proposal; not evidence]** Testing both down-regulation and
  up-regulation in a human microglial co-culture directly measures direction;
  perturbation pairs directly test context/dependency instead of inferring it
  from correlation.

The fixed parameter should be **direction-resolved functional rescue**, not a
particular favorite target. Candidate identities remain flexible until a
predeclared selection audit establishes why each belongs in the pilot.

## Two-Stage Pilot

### Stage 1: Direction and construct calibration

1. Use independent human iPSC donors and a microglia-containing CNS co-culture.
2. For every candidate, run CRISPRi and CRISPRa, multiple guides, non-targeting
   controls, and positive technical controls.
3. Predeclare orthogonal outcomes: transcript state, phagocytosis/myelin-debris
   handling, lipid/oxidative stress, and oligodendrocyte or neuronal support.
4. Require guide concordance, donor replication, dose/direction coherence, and
   absence of generic toxicity before any candidate enters Stage 2.

No existing project result determines that any named candidate will pass.

### Stage 2: Interaction-efficient screen

- **[external-unverifiable; source: `knowledge_external/records/method_compressed_perturbseq_2024.json`]** Compressed Perturb-seq provides published method
  context for reducing measurement cost and learning interactions.
- **[external-unverifiable; source: `knowledge_external/records/method_active_intervention_design_2024.json`]** Active intervention design provides method context
  for choosing informative perturbations.
- **[grounded-method; synthetic only]** In 9,000 seeded synthetic screens, the
  frozen D-optimal 36-pair design met its cross-seed RMSE/rank gate when pair
  effects were descriptor-aligned or mixed, but rank/top-hit advantage vanished
  for idiosyncratic interactions. Source:
  `analysis/v57_perturbation_design/REPORT.md`.

Therefore, run a random-design comparator and hold out perturbations. If
descriptor generalization fails, abandon optimal/compressed expansion and use
direct arrayed confirmation; do not let the model nominate evidence.

## Downstream Tissue Context

- **[external-unverifiable; source: `knowledge_external/records/method_in_vivo_neuroinflammation_crispr_2026.json`, `knowledge_external/records/method_spatial_perturbseq_2026.json`]** In vivo CRISPR screening in a mouse
  neuroinflammation model and Spatial Perturb-Seq in intact mouse brain provide
  technical precedents for later tissue-context testing.
- **[method decision; not evidence]** These are not first experiments. They
  require a replicated human-cell perturbation, a species-transfer
  falsification criterion, donor/animal replication, and a predeclared spatial
  negative-control family.

## Kill Criteria

Stop or redesign the route if any holds:

1. CRISPRi/a direction is not concordant across guides and donors.
2. Transcript changes do not reproduce in orthogonal functional readouts.
3. Effects are explained by viability, cell-cycle, or batch controls.
4. Pair effects are not predicted in held-out perturbations better than the
   random-design comparator.
5. A human-cell result reverses or disappears in the next context model.

## Honest Decision

The leading new method is not a newly claimed MS target. It is a staged,
direction-resolving human perturbation program that can produce the causal and
context evidence the current repository lacks. Its first executable unit is a
small CRISPRi/a calibration pilot; spatial or in vivo expansion is contingent,
not assumed.
