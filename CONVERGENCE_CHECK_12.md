# Convergence Check 12

Timestamp: 2026-05-27 07:53 UTC

## Forcing Question

After static upstream/niche axes failed, does a dynamic transition-controller
analysis identify a druggable way to decouple HLA-II/CD74/GILT antigen
presentation from generic IFN/JAK host-defense collapse?

## What The Tracks Believe

- Cross-disease cell-state track: the central recurrent state remains an
  IFN/HLA-II/CD74/GILT antigen-presentation and lipid-lysosomal APC/tissue-state
  program. This is robust as a disease-state observation, not yet a target.
- Perturbation track: `MED16_KO` in primary mouse macrophages is the strongest
  selective suppression comparator for the antigen-presentation module relative
  to generic IFN genes.
- Druggability/translation track: `MED16` itself has no direct druggable handle.
  The plausible target-neighborhood route, CDK8/CDK19 Mediator kinase
  inhibition, lacks direct autoimmune APC phenocopy and is prior-art saturated.
- L1000 track: multiple compounds reverse antigen-presentation-like signatures
  in cell lines, but these are unsupported by immune-cell perturbation data and
  are often cytotoxic, oncology/stress, or broadly inflammatory.

## Agreement

All tracks agree that the module is biologically real and recurrent, but direct
suppression of the module is not a defensible therapeutic claim under the V3
DoD.

## Disagreement / Tension

The main tension is whether `MED16_KO` should be treated as sufficient to
promote the Mediator kinase route. The answer remains no: the chemical handle is
not the perturbation that produced the effect, and prior Wave17 data did not
show a CDK8/CDK19 inhibitor phenocopy in the relevant APC context.

## Decision

Close the direct dynamic-controller branch. Pivot to downstream resolution and
tissue-repair biology: efferocytosis, lipid clearance, lysosomal repair,
resolution macrophage switching, and remyelination-compatible inflammatory
resolution. The new forcing question is whether a cross-autoimmune node can
make the lipid-lysosomal inflammatory state resolve rather than simply
suppressing IFN/HLA-II signaling.
